Transit Pal

Tourist Confidence System for NYC Transit Navigation

Consumer App Transit & Navigation UX Research

Problem

NYC tourists struggle with unpredictable delays, city-specific terminology that existing apps don't explain, and a lack of trustworthy local recommendations, leaving them feeling anxious and unsupported throughout their trip.

Team

Solo

Role

UX/UI Designer

Duration

Jun – Aug 2025

Key Outcome

Designed a tourist-first NYC transit app with Safe Arrival mode for delay recovery, a NavBot AI assistant for real-time guidance, simplified fare and payment flows, and curated local recommendations, giving visitors a complete support system for navigating the city on their own.

What I Drove

UX Research User Interviews Competitive Analysis Information Architecture Wireframing High-Fidelity Mockups

Product

Transit Pal is a tourist-focused NYC transit app featuring Safe Arrival mode, curated local recommendations, and an integrated AI chatbot that helps visitors navigate the city with confidence, even when things go wrong.

Goal

Design a tourist-centered transit experience that helps visitors understand routes, recover from delays, decode city-specific transit language, and discover trustworthy local recommendations.

Tools & Skills

01 Research

Tourists face unique challenges when navigating a new city.

Whenever I traveled, I found myself confused by transit signs and directions in unfamiliar cities. Since I'm from New York, where even locals get lost sometimes, I suspected tourists faced unique struggles that existing navigation apps weren't solving, so I decided to look into it.

The finding was consistent across all apps: existing transit apps are designed for locals, not visitors. They give directions without context, show delays without explanation, and offer no curated path for someone exploring the city for the first time.

Interviewing tourists about their biggest frustrations.

I interviewed four non-native New Yorkers in their 20s who had previously visited NYC, and organized their frustrations into four common categories: navigation and terminology, timing and delays, payment confusion, and lack of relevant recommendations.

02 Define

These pain points pointed to a single design challenge:

How might I design a tourist-centered transit experience that helps visitors understand routes, recover from delays, decode city-specific language, and discover trustworthy local recommendations?

03 Ideation

Turning pain points into features and deciding what to build first.

Each of the four pain points mapped directly to a design solution:

Navigation & Terminology

  • NavBot — AI chat for real-time navigation help
  • Inline pop-ups that decode unfamiliar terms and highlight accessible station entrances

Timing & Delays

  • Safe Arrival mode that adds buffer time for delays, transfers, and walking
  • Accurate walking times for station exits and transfers
  • Simplified delay and alert descriptions

Payment

  • Link and reload OMNY card from the app's profile page
  • Pop-up education on payment methods when entering a station

Recommendations

  • Curated local categories on the home page
  • Tourist-specific sections — local favorites, landmarks, must-try restaurants
  • NavBot can give personalized recommendations and build itineraries mid-trip

What to features to prioritize first.

I had a lot of ideas in my head for this platform, but not every feature belongs in the first version. I separated feature that directly addressing the most pressing moments in a tourist's trip from future feature ideas that would expand the product's value over time.

MVP Features

  • Safe Arrival Mode Addresses the highest-stakes pain point: arriving late due to unaccounted delays
  • Route Warnings & Terminology Tips Decodes local/express confusion and directional language inline
  • Curated Recommendations Six tourist-relevant categories with local recommendations
  • NavBot Real-time AI assistant for emergencies, mid-trip questions, and discovery
  • OMNY Card Management Removes the kiosk dependency for payment top-ups

Future Features

  • Personalized Onboarding Record trip goals and interests to tailor recommendations from day one
  • Automatic Schedule Adjustment Proactive notifications if users haven't left in time for their next event
  • Itinerary Builder Multi-day trip planning with transit routes between each stop

04 Design

Sketches and wireframes.

Home Screen

My first home screen was built around real-time transit access, showing tourists the train and bus times nearest to them the moment they opened the app. But after digging deeper into what tourists actually needed, I realized departures alone weren't enough — they also needed help figuring out where to go in the first place. I redesigned the home screen to put recommendations and navigation directly on the page alongside transit access, better matching what tourists actually needed.

Pain Points Addressed: Navigation & Accessibility · Recommendations & Discovery

NavBot Screen

I integrated an AI chatbot to support tourists encountering unpredictable mid-trip situations — delays, missed trains, construction — where quick human help isn't available. NavBot lets users ask real-time questions in natural language and get instant, specific responses.

Directions Page

The directions page includes Safe Arrival mode, which adds buffer time for delays, walking, and missed transfers. Pop-up notifications explain city-specific terminology, delays, and payment cues directly in the flow.

Pain Points Addressed: Navigation & Accessibility · Timing & Scheduling

Profile Page & Payment

No app currently lets tourists add money to their OMNY card. I designed a profile page that links transit cards directly, allowing users to navigate and pay all in one place, removing the pre-trip and mid-trip payment anxiety entirely.

Pain Points Addressed: Payment

Recommendations Page

Tourists visit NYC for many different reasons. To avoid a one-size-fits-all result page, I organized recommendations into six categories ranging from local favorites to must-see attractions — giving different types of tourists a clear starting point without endless scrolling.

Pain Points Addressed: Recommendations & Discovery

Choosing a color palette.

My goal was to create a navigation experience that feels trustworthy, confident, and calm. I connected the design to NYC itself by pulling the primary blue from the MTA's own visual identity, making the app feel local to the system it navigates.

06 Impact

Metrics I would use to measure tourist confidence.

Each feature targets a specific anxiety point in a tourist's journey. If Transit Pal shipped, the success metrics would correlate directly to those moments, not just overall usage.

Navigation Confidence

  • Successful trip completion rate
  • Reduction in wrong-train boarding errors (through NavBot correction queries)
  • Pop-up notification engagement rate

Safe Arrival Mode

  • Percentage of users who enable Safe Arrival
  • On-time arrival rate with vs. without Safe Arrival active
  • Percentage of users who rate Safe Arrival as helpful

NavBot Effectiveness

  • Question completion rate (did users get a useful answer?)
  • Session length and amount of usage during a single trip
  • Most frequently asked questions

Recommendation Engagement

  • Click-through rate on recommendation categories
  • Percentage of users who visit a recommended location and return to the app afterward

07 Reflection

My Takeaways

What I learned: Transit Pal taught me that designing for tourists was really about designing for anxiety. The goal was never just directions; it was helping people feel confident navigating somewhere completely unfamiliar.

What I contributed: I designed Safe Arrival mode for real-time delay recovery, in-app fare payment, curated local recommendations, step-by-step transit explanations that decode NYC-specific language, and a NavBot AI assistant for on-demand guidance. Each feature targeted a specific failure point from research rather than adding generic functionality.

What I'm taking forward: I want to explore personalized onboarding that adapts to different trip goals, like a day visit versus a week-long stay. There's also an opportunity to make the itinerary smarter by automatically adjusting if someone falls behind schedule.
